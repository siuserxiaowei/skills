# Living Patch

Living Patch treats a software interface as a habitat rather than a neutral container. Dark space is allowed to breathe, while a single living signal carries the visual story. Structure comes from disciplined panels, strict alignment, and a deliberate imbalance between a quiet field and one concentrated point of life. Every edge and interval should feel meticulously crafted rather than decorated.

Color behaves like system state. Near-black establishes the working environment; electric green signals life and continuity; molten orange appears only where repair, heat, or progress occurs. The palette stays narrow so that every accent has meaning. Glow is controlled and local, with painstaking attention to contrast and legibility.

Scale creates the emotional turn. The interface remains visibly real, but the companion and its growth state receive enough visual weight to become the protagonist. Repeated grid marks, small telemetry labels, and restrained scan lines suggest an observable living system. Their rhythm must feel engineered by an expert, never like a generic gaming overlay.

Typography is sparse and decisive. One human sentence explains the premise; small monospaced labels provide evidence and context. Words do not compete with the real product image. The hierarchy depends on master-level spacing, exact line breaks, and countless small refinements rather than additional copy.

The final composition should feel like a carefully preserved field record of a tiny creature living inside a coding tool. It balances playfulness with technical credibility and makes the real interface the proof. Every detail, from the glow radius to the terminal labels, must show patient, top-of-field craftsmanship.
