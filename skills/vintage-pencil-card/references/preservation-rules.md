# Preservation rules

Use these rules to decide what must remain invariant before adding style.

## Person

Lock identity before aesthetics:

- face outline, forehead, jaw, cheeks, hairline, hair silhouette and part;
- eye shape, spacing and direction; brow shape; nose and mouth placement; ears;
- expression, apparent age, skin tone, and distinctive marks;
- body proportions, pose, limb overlap, hands, feet, clothing silhouette, collar, folds, and key patterns.

Background can be replaced when it is not identity-bearing. The face is the most detailed layer. Broad crayon never defines facial features.

Common failure: a prettier but generic face. Correction: name five to eight concrete facial anchors and say that identity outranks style.

## Pet

Lock species and individual markings:

- head-to-muzzle ratio, eye placement, ear shape and angle;
- coat length and exact patch boundaries;
- chest, paws, tail, collar, pose, and expression.

Common failure: a generic cute pet. Correction: restate patch locations and anatomical ratios; prohibit enlarged eyes and shortened muzzle.

## Landscape

Lock spatial topology:

- horizon height and camera elevation;
- major silhouette order from foreground to distance;
- shoreline, river, road, ridge, and reflection paths;
- foreground anchors, atmospheric depth, weather, time of day, and light direction.

The environment is the subject. Redraw it in pencil; do not replace it with blank paper. Decorative marks belong at outer edges and must not obscure terrain.

Common failure: a pleasant but different place. Correction: name the path of the water or road, relative position of landforms, and horizon height.

## Architecture

Lock geometry:

- viewpoint, vanishing directions, verticals, massing and roofline;
- floor count, openings, facade bays, entrances, stairs, and signage location;
- proportions between building and street or landscape.

Common failure: invented windows or distorted perspective. Correction: state the exact opening count and alignments; ask to simplify texture only.

## Still life and products

Lock inventory and arrangement:

- object count, silhouette, proportions, overlap order and camera angle;
- color blocks, material cues, handles, fasteners, openings, and functional details.

Common failure: objects merge or accessories appear. Correction: enumerate the objects from left to right and prohibit additions.

## Mixed scenes

Choose one primary subject and preserve its rules first. Add only the environmental anchors required to recognize the scene. Do not give every element equal detail: focal identity first, structural context second, texture last.

## Orientation changes

When converting vertical to horizontal or the reverse:

1. Identify the visual path or pose that must remain.
2. Define which edges may crop.
3. Preserve the primary subject at a readable scale.
4. Use negative space or paper, not invented scenery, to fill the new frame.

If those conditions cannot coexist, keep the source orientation and explain why.
